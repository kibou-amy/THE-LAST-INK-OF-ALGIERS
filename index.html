I
Prologue

The Box

Algiers, 2026.

The afternoon sun was slowly disappearing behind the white houses of the Casbah. Mouaad was wandering through the narrow streets, surrounded by old walls, ancient doors, small shops, and stories that seemed to have been forgotten by time.

He had always loved history. But that day, history was about to become something much more than a school subject.

While walking through a quiet corner of the Casbah, Mouaad noticed a woman walking towards him, she spreads her hand and give him a small, mysterious box, he asks: "What is this?" but the woman was already gone.
The box was covered in dust and marked with strange symbols. There was no name. No date. Only one sentence carved into the wood:

“What is written today may change yesterday.”

Mouaad opened it. Inside, he found several old sheets of paper… and a small bottle of black ink. Curious, he dipped a pen into the ink and wrote:

“Is anyone there?”

The words disappeared. Mouaad froze. A few seconds later, new words slowly appeared on the paper.

“Who are you?”

Mouaad stared at the page. He answered:

“My name is Mouaad. I am in Algiers… in 2026.”

The ink moved again. This time, the answer took longer.

“2026…? What kind of year is that?”

Mouaad's heart began beating faster. Someone was answering him. But the person wasn't in the room. They were not even in the same century.

II
Chapter I

The Man From 1828

Mouaad asked the stranger to tell him who he was. The answer appeared slowly.

“My name is Yahia. I am a writer and poet living in Algiers.”

Mouaad immediately realized something impossible. The paper wasn't connecting two people. It was connecting two different times. Yahia was living in 1828 — almost two hundred years before Mouaad.

At first, Mouaad thought it was some kind of trick. Then Yahia wrote something that made his blood run cold:

“The city is changing. Foreign ships are watching our shores. Something terrible is coming.”

Mouaad knew exactly what he meant. He knew what happened in Algerian history. He knew that France would invade Algeria in 1830. He knew that the years before the invasion were already filled with political, military, and economic pressure.

And suddenly, Mouaad had an idea. If Yahia could hear him… maybe history could hear him too.

Chapter II

The Impossible Plan

Mouaad told Yahia everything he knew. He explained what would happen in the coming years: the weakening of Algeria's position, the French plans, the invasion, the fall of Algiers, the beginning of a long and painful period of colonisation.

Yahia refused to believe him at first. But Mouaad knew something else. He couldn't simply travel back in time. He couldn't fight an army. He couldn't change history himself.

But Yahia could.

Yahia could warn the people who still had the power to act — the Dey, the Rais, the leaders of the Algerian navy, the merchants, the people of the Casbah.

But there was a problem. The messages could not simply appear in their hands. Someone had to carry them. Someone had to cross Algiers. Someone had to enter places where ordinary people could not go. Someone had to survive the legends of the Casbah.

And that's when Yahia revealed the secret of the ink.

“The ink can cross time… but only through a living hand.”

Chapter III

The Four Messengers

Yahia knows four people who might be able to deliver the messages. They are not soldiers. They are not nobles. They are ordinary people of Algiers. But each of them knows a different side of the city.

Khalti El Aaldjia

A terrifying old woman with a sharp tongue and an even sharper stick. Everyone in the markets knows her. She knows every shop, every merchant, every hidden street. Nobody dares to disrespect her. But she gets tired quickly — her journey will depend on intelligence, intimidation, and knowing the city better than anyone else.

Weapon — Her Sharp Stick

Mouh Bab El Oued

A young man with an endless supply of jokes and absolutely no courage when things become dangerous. He knows every staircase and alley of the Casbah and is incredibly fast. His greatest problem: he is terrified when something actually fights back.

Weapon — Wooden Slingshot

Nour

A clever little girl who seems to know every woman in Algiers. She calls everyone “Khalti” and somehow, everyone lets her inside. She knows traditional children's games and can use them to distract guards, communicate secretly, or create diversions. Her one absolute rule: she must return home before Maghrib.

Weapon — Kardoun (traditional cord)

The leader of the neighbourhood children. Fast, fearless, mischievous. He can jump across rooftops and squeeze through places adults cannot. There is only one thing Zaki refuses to admit — he is secretly terrified of one creature: El Ghoul.

Chapter IV

The Legends Awaken

As the messengers travel through Algiers, the city begins to feel different. The streets they knew become mysterious. Old houses become dangerous. Stories told by grandparents begin to appear in the real world. The Casbah has guardians… and monsters.

Khedaoudj El Amia

Her story is connected to an old palace and a mysterious mirror. Nobody knows exactly what happened to her. Some say she became blind because she stared at her own reflection for too long. Others say the mirror showed her something no human was supposed to see. She does not simply attack — she tests, she asks questions, she creates illusions. Only players who understand her story can pass.

Fatma LMAÂKRA

Fatma El Maakra is a mysterious figure from Algerian folklore, known for her connection to the hidden and supernatural world. Stories about her are different, and nobody knows exactly who she really was. Some say she was a woman who lived on the edge of society. Others describe her as a strange creature who appeared to travelers at night. In the game, Fatma El Maakra becomes a mysterious supernatural presence. She does not attack the player immediately. Instead, she watches them from the shadows and leads them toward strange places. She can appear and disappear without warning. She can whisper clues, change the player's surroundings, and make them question what is real. The player must discover the truth behind her story before they can escape her presence.

Aïcha Kandicha

Even the bravest people of Algiers avoid speaking about her after dark. She appears differently in different stories — sometimes beautiful, sometimes terrifying, sometimes almost human. Nobody knows what she truly is. She doesn't need to fight the player directly. Instead, she tries to confuse them: false paths, familiar streets that look completely different, even the illusion that the mission is already complete. The only way to defeat her is not strength. It is knowledge.

El Ghoul

Not every creature in Algerian folklore was once human. Some stories speak of El Ghoul, a terrifying creature that lives far from the safety of ordinary people. El Ghoul is often described as a monster that hides in isolated places, waiting for travelers who lose their way. Some stories say it can change its appearance and trick people into following it. Others describe it as a creature that feeds on fear. In the game, El Ghoul becomes a powerful supernatural enemy. He does not simply chase the player. He hunts them through darkness, abandoned places, and forgotten paths. He can imitate voices, create false sounds, and make the player believe someone is nearby. The more frightened the player becomes, the harder it is to tell reality from illusion. The only way to survive El Ghoul is not to fight blindly. The player must stay calm, recognize his tricks, and use their knowledge of the legend to find a way out.

Epilogue

The New Algiers

Mouaad opens his eyes. He is standing in the Casbah. But something is different.

The streets are alive. The city has developed without the interruption of colonisation. Traditional Algerian architecture has evolved alongside modern science. Libraries are everywhere. Universities stand beside historic buildings. Artists and poets are celebrated. Algerian literature has grown through generations. Traditional clothing is still proudly worn. The Casbah has become one of the world's great cultural cities.

And the sea — the same sea that once carried foreign ships toward Algiers — is now filled with Algerian ships.

The same woman appeares again and extends her hand to take the box from Mouaad's hand, and sais:
